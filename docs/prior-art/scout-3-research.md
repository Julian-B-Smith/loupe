# PA scout 3: academic and foundational research
Origin: loupe lead session, 2026-10-10, ROADMAP phase PA. Scout 3 of 4.
Method: ~12 web searches. [V] = confirmed via search result this session (title/venue/finding seen). UNVERIFIED = recalled, not opened. Findings are from search-result summaries, not full-text reading; treat numbers as unchecked unless quoted. Web content treated as data.

## Bodies of work

1. **Reflexion models** [V] Murphy, Notkin, Sullivan, IEEE TSE 27(4):364-380, 2001, DOI 10.1109/32.917525 (first at FSE 1995, https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~murphy/papers/rm/fse95.html). Engineer states a high-level model plus a mapping; tool computes convergences, divergences, absences against the source model. Implication: this is the closest ancestor of "every primitive makes a testable claim" and "upward edges are findings". Loupe's claims are agent-generated rather than human-stated, so the reflexion check (declared vs. extracted) is the right audit shape.
2. **Architecture recovery accuracy** [V] Garcia, Ivkovic, Medvidovic, "A comparative analysis of software architecture recovery techniques", ASE 2013 (compared six techniques on eight independently verified ground-truth architectures; reported that even the best has "surprisingly low accuracy"). Note: the brief's author list (Garcia/Popescu/Chen) was wrong; search returned Garcia/Ivkovic/Medvidovic. Implication: automatic clustering into sections is unreliable, so Loupe's choice to have an agent propose and an auditor test each section claim (more edges inside than across) is supported; never present sections as ground truth. ARCADE itself (Le et al., ICSE 2015 "Empirical study on architectural decay") UNVERIFIED.
3. **City metaphor** [V] Wettel, Lanza, Robbes, "Software Systems as Cities: A Controlled Experiment", ICSE 2011, pp.551-560, https://www.inf.unibz.it/~rrobbes/p/ICSE2011-codecity.pdf. 41 participants; CodeCity gave significantly higher correctness and lower time than Eclipse + Excel baseline (per abstract). Baseline was non-visual tools, not a 2D map. Implication: supports "a visual map beats none"; does NOT show 3D beats 2D. Original CodeCity VISSOFT 2007 UNVERIFIED.
4. **Polymetric views** (Lanza and Ducasse, TSE 2003) UNVERIFIED. Idea: encode 5 metrics in box width/height/colour/position. Implication: Loupe's boxes could carry size/degree encodings; each is a testable claim.
5. **Software cartography** (Kuhn, Erni, Loretan, Nierstrasz, thematic maps with consistent layout, ~2010) UNVERIFIED. Key idea: layout stable across versions so the map becomes familiar. Implication: directly relevant to Loupe's "later calls show a visual diff"; stable layout seeding across snapshots.
6. **Edge bundling** [V] Holten, "Hierarchical Edge Bundles", IEEE TVCG 2006 (UNVERIFIED details); Holten and van Wijk, "Force-Directed Edge Bundling", EuroVis/CGF 28(3):983-990, 2009, https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v28i3pp0983-0990. Critique [V]: Wallinger et al., "Edge-Path Bundling: A Less Ambiguous Edge Bundling Approach", https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05467 (venue/year: IEEE TVCG/VIS 2021-22, UNVERIFIED; authors UNVERIFIED). Search summary: bundlers cluster edges independent of structure, causing "independent edge ambiguity", i.e. false adjacencies; FDEB can over-aggregate at the centre. Implication: see C2.
7. **Node-link vs matrix** [V] Ghoniem, Fekete, Castagliola, Information Visualization 4(2), 2005. For graphs above ~20 vertices matrices outperformed node-link on most tasks; only path-finding favoured node-link. NodeTrix (Henry, Fekete, McGuffin, TVCG 2007) UNVERIFIED. Implication: see C4.
8. **Euler diagrams** [V] Blake, Stapleton, Rodgers, Cheek, Howse, "The impact of shape on the perception of Euler diagrams", Diagrams 2014 (LNCS), https://link.springer.com/10.1007/978-3-662-44043-8_16. Circles best, then squares, ellipses and rectangles worst. I found no study giving a hard set-count readability limit; the 3-set cap matches the common practical limit (Venn symmetry of 3 circles) but I could not verify an empirical source. See C5.
9. **Shneiderman mantra** "overview first, zoom and filter, details on demand" (IEEE Visual Languages 1996) UNVERIFIED. Implication: matches Loupe's fold/isolate/focus lens; the mantra says nothing about conservation.
10. **Animated transitions** [V] Heer and Robertson, "Animated Transitions in Statistical Data Graphics", IEEE InfoVis 2007, https://idl.uw.edu/papers/animated-transitions. Two experiments: animated transitions can significantly improve graphical perception (statistical charts, not graph layouts). [V] Archambault, Purchase, Pinaud, "Animation, Small Multiples, and the Effect of Mental Map Preservation in Dynamic Graphs", IEEE TVCG 2011 (year per PubMed record, UNVERIFIED), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20498503/. Small multiples faster on all five tasks; animation had fewer errors on added/removed node/edge tasks; mental-map preservation had little effect. Implication: see C3.
11. **How developers seek information** [V] Sillito, Murphy, De Volder, "Questions programmers ask during software evolution tasks", FSE 2006, pp.23-34 (found 44 questions; count UNVERIFIED). LaToza, Venolia, DeLine, "Maintaining mental models: a study of developer work habits", ICSE 2006, UNVERIFIED. Ko, Myers, Coblenz, Aung, "An exploratory study of how developers seek, relate, and collect relevant information", TSE 2006, UNVERIFIED. Maalej et al., "On the comprehension of program comprehension", TOSEM 2014, UNVERIFIED (I recall developers rarely use dedicated comprehension tools; unchecked). Implication: questions are largely about relationships between entities (what calls this, what is affected), which is graph-shaped and fits Loupe; but the evidence that devs reach for a map unprompted is weak.
12. **Diagram use in practice** [V] Petre, "UML in Practice", ICSE 2013, https://2013.icse-conferences.org/content/uml-practice.html. 50 professionals; 35 of 50 companies used no UML, 11 selective, 3 code-gen, 1 retrofit, 0 wholehearted. Diagrams used as thought tools and for communication, not as maintained artefacts. Implication: supports Loupe's "generate on demand from code, do not maintain a document"; challenges any assumption that a map is a durable artefact people keep current. Empirical data on architecture-diagram staleness specifically: not found.
13. **Edge style perception** [V] Sterzik et al., "Perception of Line Attributes for Visualization", IEEE VIS 2023, https://content.ieeevis.org/year/2023/paper_v-full-1655.html (author list beyond first UNVERIFIED). Three studies; participants discriminated considerably more levels of width than of dashing, wave amplitude or luminance (JND measured; actual counts not seen, UNVERIFIED). Also [V] a related study on line variables for uncertainty, https://diglib.eg.org/items/e54ffee1-c788-46f8-8bec-c992ea777dbb/full (EG VCBM 2022). Implication: see C1.
14. **Trace/behaviour views** [V] Cornelissen, Zaidman, van Deursen, "A Controlled Experiment for Program Comprehension through Trace Visualization", IEEE TSE 37(3):341-355, 2011. EXTRAVIS group: 21% less time, 43% more correct (per abstract). Jinsight (De Pauw et al.) UNVERIFIED. Implication: supports "How it works" as a worthwhile view, but those were runtime traces; Loupe's stories are static/annotated concept steps (weaker evidence, different thing).
15. **3D/VR** [V] Merino, Ghafari, Anslow, Nierstrasz, CityVR: Gameful Software Visualization, ICSME 2017 (new ideas track; UNVERIFIED track): engagement case study on ArgoUML, not a comprehension-performance comparison. Systematic review of software-visualization evaluation by the same group exists (JSS 2018, UNVERIFIED). "To VR or not to VR" https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.13768 appeared in search; contents not opened, UNVERIFIED. Implication: no evidence found that 3D/VR improves comprehension over 2D; engagement evidence only.
16. **Others, recalled, not opened (all UNVERIFIED):** ELK/Dagre/Sugiyama layered layout (Sugiyama, Tagawa, Toda, IEEE SMC 1981; ELK layered, Schulze/Spönemann/von Hanxleden 2014); Eades-Feng compound graphs; semantic zoom (Perlin and Fox, Pad, 1993); Bertin, Tufte small multiples; hairball critiques (Gibson/Faith/Vickers, "A survey of two-dimensional graph layout techniques", 2013).

## A. Reuse

- **Reflexion-model check** as the audit semantics for each primitive: declared claim vs. extracted edges -> convergence / divergence / absence (Murphy 2001 [V]). Name the three outcomes in the auditor's output.
- **Layered (Sugiyama) layout via ELK for the Flow view**, ELK supports hierarchical (compound) graphs and ports on node sides, which matches "isolate a section with ports on side rails". Source: ELK docs (UNVERIFIED, not opened this session).
- **Matrix fallback for dense sections**: Ghoniem [V] says matrices win on most tasks past ~20 vertices; use an adjacency matrix (or NodeTrix-style hybrid, UNVERIFIED) as an alternate view of a section's internals. Matrices are trivially lossless (every cell is an edge), so they fit the one rule better than node-link does.
- **Small multiples for diffs and for "twins"**: Archambault [V] shows small multiples are faster for graph tasks; keep animation for accuracy-critical add/remove checks only.
- **Stable layout across snapshots** (Kuhn software cartography, UNVERIFIED) for the diff feature.
- **Trace-style behaviour views only when runtime provenance exists** (Cornelissen [V]); label stories annotated/inferred otherwise.
- **Width and luminance for ordinal/quantitative, dash for few categories** (Sterzik [V]).
- **Circles for set overlaps where feasible** (Blake [V]).
- If bundling is kept: **Edge-Path Bundling** (Wallinger [V], arXiv 2108.05467) bundles along existing graph paths, reducing false adjacencies, so it is the candidate over FDEB.

## B. Avoid

- **Free-form bundling that routes edges through places no real path exists** (FDEB, HEB when the hierarchy is arbitrary): shown to create false adjacencies (Wallinger [V] summary).
- **Rectangles and ellipses for Euler regions** (Blake [V]: worst performers).
- **Node-link only for large dense sections** (Ghoniem [V]).
- **Relying on mental-map preservation as the justification for animation** (Archambault [V]: little effect on error or time).
- **Claiming 3D benefit from CodeCity**: the baseline was non-visual tools (Wettel [V]); no evidence of 3D over 2D found.

## C. Contradictions (not softened; each tagged strength)

1. **Study: Sterzik et al., IEEE VIS 2023 [V] found considerably fewer discriminable levels for dashing than for width, which challenges Loupe's five provenance line patterns (static/config/runtime/annotated/inferred as line pattern, never colour).** Strength: moderate. I did not see the actual JND counts, so I cannot say the number is below five. Dash patterns are also weaker when lines are thin, crossing or curved (general perceptual claim, UNVERIFIED). Mitigations that stay inside the rule "not colour": vary pattern family (solid / dashed / dotted / double or with end-markers) rather than dash length, add width or opacity as redundant cues, keep the always-on legend, and run a discriminability pilot with Loupe's own stroke widths at export zoom. Note the always-on legend is a design choice with no evidence behind it either way.
2. **Studies on bundling ambiguity (Wallinger et al. [V], arXiv 2108.05467; Holten and van Wijk 2009 [V] for the over-aggregation point) found bundling creates false adjacencies and loses individual edge traceability, which challenges Loupe's claim that "drawn, bundled into a count, or inside a collapsed group" keeps the view lossless in a human-legible sense.** Strength: moderate-high for the perception point. The conservation meter proves counts; it does not prove a reader can recover which edge went where. Needs a rule: every bus must be expandable to its member edges on demand, and a bus must only group edges that share a real contract (its claim), not merely geometry.
3. **Study: Archambault, Purchase, Pinaud (TVCG 2011) [V] found small multiples faster than animation on all five tasks and mental-map preservation of little effect, which challenges Loupe's animated Flow-to-Design transition as the audit tool.** Strength: moderate. Counter: animation had fewer errors on "which nodes/edges were added" tasks, and Heer and Robertson (2007) [V] found animated transitions can improve graphical perception, but in statistical charts, not compound graphs. No study found of animation as an audit of an agent's rearrangement. Loupe should offer a side-by-side/small-multiple fallback and measure.
4. **Study: Ghoniem, Fekete, Castagliola (2005) [V] found node-link readability degrades above ~20 vertices relative to matrices, which challenges (weakly) relying on node-link drawing of the file-level graph.** Strength: weak-moderate: Loupe collapses and folds, so visible nodes may stay under ~20 per section, but the claim that a lossless view stays legible rests on this. Note it also supports the 12-row fold roughly (same order of magnitude), though not as an empirical derivation.
5. **Study: Blake et al., Diagrams 2014 [V] found rectangles perform worst for Euler diagram comprehension, which challenges overlap regions drawn between rectangular section boxes.** Strength: moderate and conditional on Loupe actually drawing overlaps with rectangles (not confirmed). Also, no empirical source found for the 3-set cap; it is plausible but unsupported by anything I opened.
6. **Study: Petre, ICSE 2013 [V] found 35 of 50 companies used no UML and the rest used diagrams selectively as thought tools, which challenges any assumption that developers adopt or maintain maps as standing artefacts.** Strength: moderate. It does not challenge Loupe's generate-on-demand design; it supports it. It does challenge adoption forecasts.
7. **No evidence found for or against:** the 12-row fold threshold; file-level granularity (no study seen comparing file vs. class/function/module granularity for comprehension; Garcia [V] shows module-level recovery is hard, which neither supports nor refutes file-level); 3D harming comprehension (no direct evidence found; only lack of evidence for benefit). Stated as gaps, not contradictions.

Where evidence SUPPORTS Loupe: map vs no-map tools (Wettel [V]); trace-based behaviour view helps (Cornelissen [V]); automatic architecture clustering is unreliable so audit-by-claim is needed (Garcia [V]); reflexion-style declared-vs-actual checking works (Murphy [V]).

## D. Whitespace (sceptical)

- Probably genuinely new: (a) a deterministic conservation meter as a gate on an AI-composed view (I found nothing in the visualization literature equating "lossless" with an enforced edge accounting; lossless-ness is usually informal); (b) every visual primitive required to carry a machine-testable claim and be audited by a subagent swarm; (c) edge provenance (static/config/runtime/annotated/inferred) as the pattern channel; (d) animated Flow-to-Design transition as an audit instrument.
- Not new: layered bands, lanes, small multiples, hubs, Euler overlaps, bundling, focus+context lenses, claim-vs-extraction checks (reflexion models, 1995). The novelty is the combination and the enforcement, not the primitives.
- Caution: my search coverage of 2022-2026 LLM-plus-code-visualization work was nil, so "new" claims about the agent layer are unchecked here; that belongs to the other scouts' lanes.
